The guide

Follow the paper trail, then rehearse every step.

A site visit tests whether three sources agree: what ADS says, what the files show, and what people say in the room. When they disagree, findings follow, even when the program runs well.

Why mock visits matter now

The ten-year program site visit is gone.

2023

October 2023: ACGME discontinued the program 10-Year Accreditation Site Visit.

2025

July 1, 2025: Field Representatives and Review Committees no longer ask for or review Self-Study information.

9+ yrs

Programs on Continued Accreditation are selected by random sampling once nine or more years pass without a visit.

30 days

Approximate minimum notice for any accreditation site visit.

Source: ACGME Site Visit FAQs

Miss the ADS update due date in the Site Visit Announcement letter and you risk a postponed visit and a $3,200 fine, per ACGME's ADS checklist. Build ADS cleanup into your mock timeline, well ahead of any real deadline.

Required versus chosen

Know what ACGME requires and what you choose.

ACGME requirements

  • Update ADS by the Site Visit Announcement due date
  • Upload the signed Site Visit Attestation Statement
  • Upload the documents ACGME lists for your status
  • Peer-selected resident and fellow representatives in larger programs
  • GMEC oversight of accreditation status (IR 1.12.a in 2026; IR 1.9.a.1 on July 1, 2027)

Local practice

  • Mock site visits
  • Your mock agenda and mock reviewers
  • Your internal question bank
  • Your debrief format and GMEC reporting

A mock visit gives your GMEC a practical way to show oversight in action.

Site visit types

Eight reasons ACGME schedules a site visit.

Application

New program application.

Initial Accreditation

End of the Initial Accreditation period, before a decision on Continued Accreditation.

Annual data review

Review Committee request after its review of ADS data: surveys, Case Logs, board pass rates, scholarly activity, attrition, program changes.

Random sampling

Continued Accreditation, nine or more years since the last visit.

SI 10-Year

Sponsoring Institution, after its Self-Study process.

Probation

Required before the next status decision.

Complaint

Review of complaint allegations.

Other

Review Committee request at its discretion.

Start by asking which of these is most likely for the program in front of you. A program nearing the end of Initial Accreditation needs a different mock than a program with a long record of Continued Accreditation.

How a real visit unfolds

Six steps from blackout dates to the Letter of Notification.

1

Blackout date email

About 8 dates per 30-day window in ADS. Locked once submitted. ACGME decides remote or in person.

2

Site Visit Announcement

Date, format, Field Representative, and ADS due dates.

3

Letter of Instruction

Schedule, strengths and areas-for-improvement lists, extra documents.

4

ADS updates and uploads

Complete by the due date, including the signed attestation.

5

Site visit day

Interviews, document review, concluding meeting.

6

Report and decision

Factual Site Visit Report to the Review Committee. Letter of Notification in ADS 60 to 90 days after the committee meets.

Field Representatives collect information. Review Committees make accreditation decisions. Rehearse every step, starting with a practice letter.

Who ACGME asks to attend

Schedule the same people ACGME expects.

Program visit

  • Program director
  • Program Administrator(s)
  • DIO and/or chair, or a representative
  • Faculty members
  • Residents/fellows

Small programs: all residents on duty that day. Larger programs: peer-selected from all required years; the most senior year often meets separately.

Institutional visit

  • DIO and Institutional Manager(s)
  • Program directors and Program Administrators
  • GMEC members
  • Senior institutional officials, such as the CEO
  • Residents/fellows representing programs
  • Faculty members representing all sites

The Field Representative often meets briefly with the Program Administrator or Institutional Manager. Practice your own interview too.

Program mock visit

Build a program mock visit in five steps.

1

Choose the scenario

Match the visit type and the program's accreditation status.

2

Build the mock team

Lead reviewer from outside the program: GME office, a peer program in another specialty, a sister institution, or a former site visitor. Ground rules in writing.

3

Gather the sources

Latest application or ADS data, last Letter of Notification, citations, survey results.

4

Mirror the schedule

Same interview groups and order ACGME uses.

5

Debrief and track

Every finding gets an owner, a due date, and a status.

Mock timeline

Give every mock a six-week runway.

Weeks 6 to 5

Audit

Trace ADS to source documents. Freeze discretionary ADS changes.

Week 4

Assemble

Build the document set in the order a Field Representative asks for it. Name one person who knows where every item lives.

Weeks 3 to 2

Prepare

Short orientation for each interview group. Topics, not scripts.

Week 1

Perform

Run mock day. Send the written report within days.

Timeline recommended by the presenters. Scale down for a focused mock.

Where most findings start

Start with ADS and trace every field to a source.

Fields to trace

  • Rosters: match faculty and resident rosters to appointment records
  • Block diagram: match it to the schedules residents work, with a key for abbreviations
  • Scholarly activity: list only what someone can produce on request
  • Citation responses: describe what the program does today, not last year's plan
  • Major changes: state what changed and the improvement it supports

ACGME Field Activities flags

  • Consistency across documents
  • Block diagram and rosters
  • Responses to citations and major changes
  • Faculty scholarly activity
  • Accuracy and typos

A response describing a fix the program later dropped turns a compliance gap into a credibility gap.

The heart of the mock visit

Build questions from the documents ACGME reads.

Latest application or ADS update

Build questions section by section.

Current citations

Ask about each one using ACGME's exact citation language and your response.

Areas for improvement

Pull every item from your last Letter of Notification.

Every "must"

Turn each must in the IRs, CPRs, and specialty requirements into a question.

Supervision policy

Test the written policy against CPR 6.6 to 6.9 and what residents describe, including when a supervising physician must be physically present.

ACGME survey results

Turn low-scoring Resident/Fellow and Faculty Survey items into topics.

Aim the mock at your specialty's citations. Open your Review Committee's specialty update presentation from the ACGME Annual Educational Conference and build interview questions around its frequent citations and areas for improvement. In ACGME's 2025 General Surgery update, the top five were fear of retaliation, 80 hours, faculty interest in education, service-to-education imbalance, and one day in seven. Pediatrics residency themes for 2023 to 2024: evaluations, timely faculty feedback, required language in final evaluations, and the 80-hour limit.

Institutional mock visit

Build the institutional mock around ADS uploads and six interview themes.

Site visit uploads

  • Written policies
  • Sample resident and fellow agreements of appointment
  • Professional liability coverage

Institutional Review Questionnaire

  • Org chart showing the DIO's position
  • Org chart showing the GMEC's position
  • Statement of Commitment
  • GMEC membership
  • Annotated GMEC minutes
  • Annual Institutional Review summaries
  • GMEC Special Review protocol
  • Special Review reports, if available

Six interview themes

  • GMEC oversight: how members monitor each program's accreditation status
  • Annual Institutional Review: latest LON, ACGME surveys, program statuses and citations (IR 1.14.a)
  • Special Review: underperformance criteria, report, and GMEC monitoring (IR 1.15)
  • Work hours: institutional oversight independent of program reports (IR FAQ, 3.2.e)
  • Resident forum: option, at least in part, to meet without the DIO, faculty, or administrators (IR 2.3.b)
  • Your role: learning environment, institutional support, professional development

Mock test: pick any requirement and ask, "Show me the GMEC minute."

Requirement numbers reflect the 2026 Institutional Requirements.

Coming July 1, 2027

Update your institutional mock for the 2027 Institutional Requirements.

Major revision approved by the ACGME Board on September 28, 2026, effective July 1, 2027. Six changes affect your institutional mock.

No Core, Detail, or Outcome labels

Requirement text no longer carries categories.

AIR written summary (1.10, 1.9.b.7)

Five minimum elements, reviewed and approved by the GMEC.

Special Review policy (4.2)

Initial findings to the GMEC within six months (4.2.d).

Identifiable information (1.8.c)

GMEC must not receive or discuss identifiable assessment, performance, or health information about individual residents or fellows.

Work hour monitoring (1.9.a.8)

Must not depend only on program reports.

GME administrative professionals (2.1.d, 2.2.d)

Support, dedicated time, and professional development.

Build your mock from the requirements in effect on the date of the visit.

Mock visit day

Run the mock the way ACGME runs the real visit.

Mirror the remote setup

  • Practice on Zoom, ACGME's remote platform
  • One person per device, camera on
  • Full face visible, name displayed
  • Quiet, private room
  • IT on standby

Set the ground rules

  • No recording of interviews
  • Interview privacy
  • No retaliation against residents/fellows
  • Encourage honesty, with no coaching

Facilitate like a Field Representative

  • One lead voice asks the questions
  • Observers stay silent
  • Ask the follow-up question where answers and documents disagree
  • Keep real time boxes
  • Produce documents at real-visit speed

After the mock

Turn findings into a closed plan.

Write like citations

Requirement, then evidence. Citation risks first, strengths kept. Do not soften it.

Deliver together

Same report to the program director and the DIO, within days.

Track in the APE

Owner, due date, and evidence for every item. One plan, not parallel paperwork. Re-verify before the real visit.

Report to GMEC

Closure and resource gaps, with no resident-identifiable details.

Scale it

One system for every program you support.

Triggers for scheduling a mock

  • End of Initial Accreditation approaching
  • Warning or probationary status
  • Drop in ACGME survey results
  • New program director or Program Administrator
  • Nearing nine years since the last visit

One reusable kit

  • Stakeholder workbook
  • Question bank template, organized by source document
  • Debrief tracker, same fields for every program

Trade reviewers across programs. A 2010 JGME article from Duke reported internal reviews benefited the reviewers' programs as well as the program under review.

Common questions

How far ahead should we hold a mock?

ACGME sets no timeline for mock visits. Because minimum notice for a real visit is about 30 days, hold your mock before notice arrives, not after.

Who should play the Field Representative?

Someone outside the program with strong knowledge of the requirements who will ask follow-up questions and stay neutral.

Should residents see the questions in advance?

Share the format and the topics, and skip scripted answers. ACGME's Field Activities team advises against coaching.
